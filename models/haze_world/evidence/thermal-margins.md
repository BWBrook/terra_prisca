# Methanogen thermal margins: evidence for the evolutionary variant

Codex (GPT-6), 9 November 2026. These observations justify exploring thermal
adaptation; they do not calibrate a universal 8–12 K margin, optimum floor,
20 K performance half-width, or the preview's trade-off E.
Verified bibliographic entries are in
[`haze-evolution.bib`](../../../docs/references/haze-evolution.bib).

## Laboratory temperatures and habitat temperatures

Temperatures below are °C. A measured laboratory minimum, an extrapolated
minimum, the model's resource-dependent net-growth edge, and habitat
temperature are different quantities. Optimum minus habitat temperature is
not optimum minus minimum growth temperature.

Primary full text was read for Kendall and Zhang; the Franzmann, Chong and
Chen bounds above are limited to verified primary abstracts. Unrecovered
limits are left missing. These modern isolates do not establish Archean
community composition. In particular, methylotrophs cannot directly supply
parameters for the model's H₂-consuming guild.

## What the hotter-is-better references support

[Knies, Kingsolver & Burch (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1086/597224) concerns
fitness among bacteriophages, not methanogens. It supports a possible
trade-off form rather than the numerical parameters for our organisms.
[Dell, Pawar & Savage (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1015178108) compiled
1,072 within-species responses across 309 species and 112 traits. Their mean
rising-limb activation energy is about 0.66 eV (median 0.55), but the
preview's E describes how **peak growth changes across evolving optima**.
Those are different estimands. Neither reference supplies the chosen
0.32–0.65 eV range as a measured methanogen bound.

Keep E, width and any hard floor as named sensitivities. To fit them, we need
strain-specific growth curves under controlled donor supply, maintenance
loss and acclimation, and comparable habitat measurements. Model T is a
global annual surface mean; an aquatic or sediment microsite also requires
an explicit habitat-to-climate mapping. A fit to laboratory growth alone
cannot supply that mapping or a demographic extinction threshold.
