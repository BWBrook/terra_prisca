## S1 Parameters

**Table S1** ([table_s1.md](table_s1.md), [table_s1.csv](table_s1.csv)) lists the reference parameters and the main Stage B and dial settings with value, units, basis, source and code location. Values are drawn from model objects, source tables and experiment constants, with explicit checks on cited definitions; code locations are navigation aids. The six-temperature kinetic tables, with the derived capacity, maintenance, cessation pressure and hydrogen requirement at each temperature, are in [table_s1_kinetics.md](table_s1_kinetics.md). Basis labels: source (quoted), transferred (a measurement applied beyond its system), assumption, derived, swept.

Unit conversions, with values from table S1:

- Years and hours: $H = (3.15576\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{s\,yr^{-1}})/(3600\ \mathrm{s\,h^{-1}}) = 8766\ \mathrm{h\,yr^{-1}}$. Uptake $q_i$ and maintenance $m_i$ are in mol H₂ gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹, so $q_iX_i$ is in mol L⁻¹ h⁻¹ and $H q_i X_i$ in mol L⁻¹ yr⁻¹. Equation (2.4) gives $dp/dt$ in Pa yr⁻¹ through $K_H = 7.65\times10^{-9}$ mol L⁻¹ Pa⁻¹.
- Hydrogen input from the supply Φ (CH₄-equivalent molecules cm⁻² s⁻¹), with four H₂ per CH₄ and habitat volume $V$ (L per cm² of surface):

$$J = \frac{4\,\Phi\,(3600)}{N_A V}\ \mathrm{mol\,H_2\,L^{-1}\,h^{-1}}. \tag{S1}$$

  The volume is set once so that $J = 10^{-6}$ at the reference supply: $V = 8.97068\times10^{-3}$ L cm⁻². The supply potential $P = t_{CH_4}\,c\,\Phi$ is the methane ratio the supply would sustain if all of it became methane. The reference is $P = 0.30$ ($\Phi = 3.75158\times10^{11}$); the map also uses $P = 0.24$ ($3.00126\times10^{11}$) and $0.36$ ($4.50189\times10^{11}$). The lifeless hydrogen pressure is $p_0 = p_{atm} + J/k_{ex} = 300$ Pa.
- Column constants for equation (2.6), from case B ($p_s = 10^5$ Pa, $p_{CO_2} = 1800$ Pa):

$$N_{air} = \frac{p_s N_A}{M_{air}\,g\,10^{4}} = 2.19242\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}},\qquad N_{CO_2} = \frac{p_{CO_2}}{p_s}N_{air} = 3.94636\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}, \tag{S2}$$

$$c = \frac{3.15576\times10^{7}}{N_{CO_2}} = 7.99664\times10^{-17},\qquad \chi = \frac{V N_A}{N_{CO_2}} = 1.36893\times10^{-2}\ \text{ratio per mol L}^{-1}. \tag{S3}$$

  Here $c$ converts a flux in molecules cm⁻² s⁻¹ to a ratio rate in yr⁻¹, and χ converts a local methane production in mol L⁻¹ yr⁻¹ to the same ratio rate. The atmospheric ratio $r$ is identified with surface CH₄/CO₂, which assumes well-mixed methane.

## S2 Model details

**Temperature laws.** Capacity and half-saturation come from the six assay temperatures $T_C = 4, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30$ °C (table S1). Each of $V_i(T)$ and $K_i(T)$ is interpolated linearly in $T$ between them, independently. Capacity is scaled to the 30 °C anchors,

$$s_A(T) = s_{A30}\frac{V_A(T)}{V_A(30\,°\mathrm{C})},\qquad s_M(T) = \gamma\, s_{A30}\frac{V_M(T)}{V_M(30\,°\mathrm{C})},\qquad s_{A30} = \frac{\mu_{max}}{Y_A} + m_{A30}. \tag{S4}$$

Maintenance follows one Arrhenius slope,

$$m_i(T) = m_{i30}\exp\left[-\frac{E_m}{R}\left(\frac1T - \frac1{T_{ref}}\right)\right],\qquad T_{ref} = 303.15\ \mathrm K. \tag{S5}$$

The cessation thresholds follow the reaction thermodynamics at fixed cessation energy,

$$p_{i0}^*(T) = p_{i30}^*\exp\left[\frac{\Delta H_i - \Delta G_{c,i}}{4R}\left(\frac1T - \frac1{T_{ref}}\right)\right], \tag{S6}$$

with $\Delta H_A = -270.7$ and $\Delta H_M = -253.0$ kJ per four-H₂ reaction, and $\Delta G_{c,A} = -24$ and $\Delta G_{c,M} = -28$ kJ per reaction. The source reports $-5$ to $-7$ (acetogens) and $-6$ to $-8$ kJ per mol H₂ (methanogens), that is $-20$ to $-28$ and $-24$ to $-32$ kJ per reaction; the chosen values lie inside both ranges. The product feedback of equation (2.8) multiplies $p_{i0}^*(T)$.

**Continuation below 277.15 K.** The reference holds $s_i$ and $K_i$ at their 277.15 K values down to the 273 K model floor (constant shape); $m_i$ and $p_i^*$ keep (S5) and (S6). The alternative continuation extends the 4 to 10 °C capacity slope,

$$s_i(T) = s_i(277.15)\exp\left[-\frac{E_i}{R}\left(\frac1T - \frac1{277.15}\right)\right],\qquad E_i = \frac{R\ln[V_i(10\,°\mathrm C)/V_i(4\,°\mathrm C)]}{1/277.15 - 1/283.15}, \tag{S7}$$

with $E_A = 53.1$ and $E_M = 116.1$ kJ mol⁻¹ and $K_i$ still frozen. The mixed state sits at $T^* = 281.53$ K, inside the measured range, so the choice changes only the methanogen-only control, which sits below 277.15 K. Table S2 gives the C06 contrast (8.02 K against 4.73 K) and the physiology each variant implies. Under the alternative, the methanogen cannot grow at 273 K at the reference loss rate ($R^*_M = \infty$).

**Catabolic flux and biomass export.** With $g_i = Y_i(q_i - m_i)$, the hydrogen equivalents retained in new biomass are $B_i = H\alpha_i (g_i)_+ X_i$ and the catabolic flux is $F_i = H X_i [q_i - \alpha_i (g_i)_+]$. Lost biomass leaves the system with its retained equivalents, at rate $E_i = H\alpha_i[\ell + (-g_i)_+]X_i$; it is not recycled to methane. Acetate and the methane source obey

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = \frac{F_A}{4} - \left(k_{rec} + k_{other}\right)C,\qquad k_{rec} = \frac{f}{t_A},\quad k_{other} = \frac{1-f}{t_A}, \tag{S8}$$

with local methane production $F_M/4 + k_{rec}C$ in mol L⁻¹ yr⁻¹. At a coexistence steady state $g_i = \ell$, so $q_i = Q_i$ and $F_i = H(Q_i - \alpha_i\ell)X_i$. The catabolised fraction is $\eta_i = 1 - \alpha_i\ell/Q_i$, which is $\eta_A = 0.894871$ and $\eta_M = 0.941322$ at the reference $T^*$ (table S1). The steady methane yields per hydrogen taken up are $f\eta_A/4$ and $\eta_M/4$. Because $\eta_A < \eta_M$ under the assumed biomass export, even $f = 1$ does not equalise the two routes (S8).

**Habitat-weighted methane source.** For habitats at offsets $\delta_j$ with quadrature weights $w_j$ ($\sum_j w_j = 1$; on a panel $[a,b]$ the Gauss–Legendre weight is $(b-a)w_{GL}/(4\sigma)$), each habitat keeps its own hydrogen, acetate and biomass balances (2.4), (S8) and (2.9) at temperature $T + \delta_j$, and the cell exchange uses the weighted mean $\bar X_i = \sum_j w_j X_{ij}$. All habitats share the atmosphere and the temperature $T$:

$$\frac{dr}{dt} = \chi\sum_j w_j\left(\frac{F_{M,j}}{4} + k_{rec}C_j\right) + c\,\Phi_{abiotic} - \frac{r}{t_{CH_4}}. \tag{S9}$$

**Climate map.** $T_{clim}(r)$ is piecewise linear through the ten extracted case-B vertices (table S1). Below the first vertex $r_0 = 0.10000663$ it follows the assumed bridge $T_{clim} = T_0 + (T_1 - T_0)\ln[1 + r_+/s]/\ln[1 + r_0/s]$ with $T_0 = 283$ K, $s = 0.01$, where $T_1$ is the temperature at $r_0$. Above the last vertex it is constant at $T_{clim} = 274.002$ K. The slope $c^{\mathsf T}$ in (2.11) is the slope of the segment containing $r$ (one-sided at vertices); at the reference switch ($r = 0.195107$) it is $-721.258$ K per unit ratio.

## S3 Numerical methods

**Root search and acceptance.** Homogeneous states are searched in the methane ratio $r$. For fixed and methane-feedback chemistry the mixed root is where $R_A^*(T_{clim}(r)) = R_M^*(T_{clim}(r))$; for acetate-feedback chemistries it is the zero of the methane balance along the mixture branch built from the other balances. The search scans a 401-point grid in $r$, brackets sign changes and refines with Brent's method to $10^{-13}$ in $r$; hydrogen, biomass and acetate then follow from linear balances. A candidate is accepted when the state-scaled residual of all balances is below $10^{-6}$, the temperature lies in the physiological domain (273 to 303.15 K; for habitats the whole support $T \pm \sigma$), and both biomasses are positive. Resolved-habitat states use a damped Newton iteration on the scaled residual with the per-capita residual of the biomass equations, accepted below $10^{-8}$ after at most 70 steps (`continuum_core.equilibrium`); the earlier equal-weight stage polished its roots to a per-capita residual of $10^{-7}$ yr⁻¹. The search is not a completeness proof; absence is reported as "none found".

**Continuation.** A habitat state is reached from the homogeneous state by raising σ in geometric steps from 0.001 K to 1 K and then in 0.5 K steps. The graded dials start from the exact homogeneous state and advance along the dial values of table S5 (D1 raises κ, D2 raises σ at fixed d). A failed step is bisected, to depth 16 for D1 and 14 for D2; a branch whose depth is exhausted is reported as unresolved, and no unresolved branch is counted as absent.

**Implicit derivative.** At a steady state $y^*$ with $F(y;\Phi) = 0$ and zero-lag Jacobian $J_0 = A + b\,c^{\mathsf T}$,

$$S = \frac{dT^*}{d\ln\Phi} = T_{clim}'(r^*)\,v_r,\qquad v = -J_0^{-1}\,\partial_{\ln\Phi}F, \tag{S10}$$

where the forcing enters only the hydrogen balance. The temperature derivative $b$ uses the five-point stencil $[F(T-2h) - 8F(T-h) + 8F(T+h) - F(T+2h)]/12h$ with $h = 10^{-4}$ K (one-sided inside $2h$ of the domain edge). S is checked against a finite supply perturbation of ±$10^{-3}$ in $\ln\Phi$ (±$10^{-4}$ on disagreement) and accepted within max($10^{-7}$ K, 2%); all 34 dial points pass (`graded_dials/prediction_assessment.json`). The frozen-composition slope $S_0$ is checked by supply perturbations of ±$10^{-4}$ in $\ln\Phi$, reduced to ±$10^{-5}$ on disagreement, with tolerance max($10^{-3}$ K, 2%).

**Characteristic equation and τ_c.** The linearisation at lag τ is $J_\tau = \begin{pmatrix} A & b\\ c^{\mathsf T}/\tau & -1/\tau\end{pmatrix}$, whose eigenvalues solve (2.11); $J_0$ is the τ = 0 limit. States are first classified by the leading real part at τ = 0: above $10^{-9}$ yr⁻¹ is already unstable (τ_c reported as 0 and listed apart), within $\pm10^{-9}$ neutral. For the others the leading real part is evaluated on the lag grid 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 yr, merged with 29 (homogeneous) or 22 (habitats) log-spaced lags from 0.001 to 10⁴ yr. A sign change from negative to positive between neighbours is refined by Brent's method (tolerance $10^{-8}$ yr homogeneous, $10^{-7}$ yr habitats) and recorded as a loss; positive to negative is recorded as a restabilisation. τ_c is the first loss. A state with no loss through 10⁴ yr is reported as "not detected through 10 000 yr", a lower bound that is never interpolated or treated as a margin. Matrices are rescaled by the state magnitudes before the eigenproblem. Habitat systems up to 2048 states use the full dense spectrum; larger ones use shift-invert Arnoldi at shifts 0, 0.001i, 0.003i, 0.01i, 0.03i, 0.1i, 0.3i and 1i yr⁻¹ (tolerance $10^{-10}$). The slow sink-population spectrum (the slowest local decay rate) is reported separately from the collective roots.

An independent route, written by Codex, eliminates the local habitat blocks by quadrature of $(\lambda I - J_j)^{-1}$ and leaves a 4 × 4 determinant in mean acetogen biomass, mean methanogen biomass, methane and temperature. It finds imaginary-axis crossings by bracketing frequency on grids of 361 and 721 points from $10^{-6}$ to 10 yr⁻¹ (`docs/audits/continuum-characteristic-2026-11-12.md`). Table S3b compares both routes on six resolved states.

**Integrators.** Trajectories use LSODA in positive coordinates (softplus for biomasses and acetate, with logarithms retained near zero so per-capita rates stay defined), relative tolerance $2\times10^{-8}$, absolute tolerances $10^{-9}$ (p, $X_A$, $X_M$), $10^{-10}$ (C), $10^{-12}$ (r) and $10^{-8}$ (T), maximum step 50 yr and output every year (`dynamic_transients.integrate`). Population minima, one-cell crossings and temperature extrema are located from the roots of the dense-output polynomials. Four selected runs (B2 at f = 0 with τ = 1 and 100 yr, and two f = 0.5, τ = 3 yr invasions: acetogen into methanogen with fixed products, methanogen into acetogen with both products) were repeated with relative tolerance $2\times10^{-9}$, maximum step 2 yr and output every 0.5 yr (`dynamic_transient_assessment.py`). The kicked runs last 30 000 yr and the invasions 100 000 yr.

**Adaptive quadrature and the doubling test (C44, C45).** Habitat spread is a uniform temperature distribution on $[-\sigma, \sigma]$. With fixed chemistry, each habitat switches from acetogen to methanogen dominance across a zone whose width scales with the cell exchange rate $d$ over the tested range; product feedback breaks that scaling (C48). Equal-weight midpoint grids do not resolve that zone: at $d = 10^{-3}$ yr⁻¹, σ = 1 K, $f = 0.5$, the critical lag changes from 7.2 yr at N = 9 to 102 yr at N = 33 and has not converged at N = 513 (782 yr); the zone width is 0.15 K at $d = 10^{-3}$ and 1.5 K at $d = 10^{-2}$ (C44; table S3a). The resolved calculation uses composite Gauss–Legendre panels of 4 nodes, starting from 4 panels, and bisects any panel across which the acetogen share changes by more than 1 in logit units. It then doubles the panels inside the mixed zone and accepts the setting only when the doubled solution agrees with the previous one: supply slopes $S$ and $S_0$ within max($10^{-3}$ K, 2%), finite τ_c within 2%, the same censoring class, and the frozen-composition slope within the same tolerance of a finite supply difference (up to 7 doublings, at most 4096 nodes). All 80 resolved C1 settings pass, with 48 to 204 nodes, and the 90 homogeneous C0 records use the same estimands (C45). Six independent characteristic checks agree: two finite margins to 2 × 10⁻⁵ relative, four as right-censored. The checks cover finite sampled branches and finite lag and frequency scans; they are not an interval proof for every root or delay (C45).

**Habitat-spread geometry (pressure-test Issue 1).** With weak exchange, every habitat colder than T\* is acetogen-held and every warmer one methanogen-held. Supply fixes the methanogen-held share that keeps the methane ratio near $r^*$, and temperature sits where the spread gives that share. For uniform offsets of ±σ the reduced closure (supply-proportional emission, linear methane removal) gives equation (3.1) of the main text,

$$\frac{1}{|S|} = \frac{1}{k} + \frac{1}{|S_{geo}|},\qquad |S_{geo}| = 2\sigma\,\frac{P_{lo}}{P}\,\frac{Q}{Q-1}, \tag{S11}$$

with $k = |dT_{clim}/dr|\,r^* = 140.72$ K per e-fold, $P_{lo} = 0.229178$ and $Q = P_{hi}/P_{lo}$ the uniform community's coexistence supply ratio (1.9955 at f = 0.5; unbounded at f = 0, where the last factor is 1). For any offset density $\rho$, $1/|S_{geo}| = \rho(T^* - T)\,\Delta$, with $\Delta$ the change in log methane yield per unit methanogen share. Against the 40 fixed-chemistry spread states above, computed/predicted $|S|$ is 0.944–1.040 for the 20 states with $d \le 10^{-3}$ yr⁻¹ and falls to 0.705 at $d = 0.1$, where exchange blends habitats (C60; `models/haze_world/review/issue1_geometric_check.py`). This is a reduced-model identity: $k$ is held at its switch value, and the hydrogen outlet (7.5% of supply at the switch) makes the endpoint $Q$ differ from the raw yield ratio, 2.104.

Finite branches at f = 0.5, fixed products and $d = 10^{-4}$ yr⁻¹ (figure 3b; C61; `models/haze_world/review/issue1_spread_response.py`) were continued from the reference supply with the production continuum solver and the S3 acceptance rules. Over the uniform coexistence interval, P = 0.229178–0.457327, temperature spans 1.911 K at σ = 1 K and 5.409 K at σ = 3 K; the resolved extrema approach T\* ± σ over slightly wider supply ranges. Branch ends are unresolved numerical stops, not ecological endpoints. The σ = 6 and 8 K branches are resolved up to P = 0.361 and 0.311, where their cold edges are within 0.03 K of the 273-K support floor. Critical response times were computed at four supplies (table S4c).

## S5 Dial brackets and the 30-year price

The two dials turn one mechanism up continuously from the exact state (graded-dial specification, [graded-dial-spec.md](../../graded-dial-spec.md)). **Coexistence interval.** At $T^*$ the mixed state needs both groups to hold the hydrogen at $R^*(T^*)$, so it exists between the supplies at which methanogens alone and acetogens alone would deliver the methane the climate state requires. With the catabolised fractions $\eta_i$ defined in S2 the supply potentials are

$$P_M = 0.30\,\frac{k_{ex}(R^* - p_{atm}) + n/\eta_M}{J_{ref}},\qquad P_A = 0.30\,\frac{k_{ex}(R^* - p_{atm}) + n/(f\eta_A)}{J_{ref}},$$

where $n = 4(r^*/t_{CH_4} - c\Phi_{abiotic})/(\chi H)$ is the catabolic hydrogen flux (mol L⁻¹ h⁻¹) that must become methane to hold $r^*$ against removal, and $R^* = p^*$ is the local hydrogen at coexistence. As f rises the interval narrows, from no upper end at f = 0 to a ratio of 1.047 at f = 1. It does not close at f = 1 because the assumed biomass export leaves a lower catabolised fraction for acetogens than for methanogens ($\eta_A < \eta_M$). If the two fractions were equal, complete acetate recycling would equalise the methane yields and close this interval. The interval is a positivity condition on the budget; stability at f = 1 was not assessed here.
