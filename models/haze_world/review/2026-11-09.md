# Review of the first methane budget

Codex, 9 November 2026. Reviewed `4070dc3`; source recovery is recorded
[separately](source-recovery.md). This review retains the conditional
equilibrium mechanism and corrects claims about dynamics and assumptions.

## The equilibrium elimination is correct

On the positive-biomass branch, setting donor and biomass derivatives to zero
gives D* = K/(ρ−1), U* = φ−k_D D* and B* = U*/δ for the implemented unit yield.
The branch is feasible when ρ > 1 + k_D K/φ. Substitution in the methane
balance gives the scalar equilibrium equation used in the draft. In physical
flux units Φ_K = K k_D/c; k_D K itself is a normalized flux per year.

This is an exact elimination at equilibrium. Setting the fast variables to
their equilibria during a trajectory is an approximation, requiring a
separate time-scale argument. The original endpoint test verifies equilibrium
agreement, not that argument.

For the reviewed case-B parameters at potential ratio 0.3, the equilibrium is
r = 0.195700698, T = 281.1065 K. The scalar relaxation rate is −0.09465 yr⁻¹.
The full ODE eigenvalues are −0.05425 and −0.03561 ± 0.14656i yr⁻¹: it has
damped oscillations of roughly 43 years rather than monotone scalar
relaxation. The frozen-methane biological modes damp on roughly 18 years.
The feedback makes the effective methane response much faster than its bare
10-kyr loss lifetime, so that lifetime comparison does not justify the scalar
transients. The Earth-mass source correction leaves this finding unchanged.

## Local stability survives without that approximation

At a positive equilibrium on a differentiable cold-growth/climate-cooling
segment, let u = ∂U/∂D > 0, h = −∂U/∂M > 0, k = k_D > 0, δ > 0 and
ℓ = 1/τ > 0. The full Jacobian's characteristic polynomial is
λ³ + a₁λ² + a₂λ + a₃, where

\[
a_1=k+u+h+\ell,\qquad
a_2=\delta u+(k+u)\ell+(k+\delta)h,\qquad
a_3=\delta u\ell+k\delta h.
\]

The coefficients are positive and a₁a₂ > a₃. These are the cubic
Routh–Hurwitz conditions for local asymptotic stability. This establishes a
conditional full-system stability result without claiming equivalent
trajectories, global attraction or empirical climate regulation. The audit
script verifies these coefficients against the numerical Jacobian.

## The original full integrator could lose rare biomass

At the same reviewed supply, start at (D,B,M) = (0.0003, 10⁻⁶, 0.2333365).
The original physical-biomass LSODA integration ended near B = −9.4×10⁻¹⁶
and r = 0.00103956, an apparent lifeless state. Its absolute tolerance loses
the dwindling population; clipping inside the derivative then makes absence
absorbing. A log-biomass integration of the same equations reaches the
positive equilibrium above.

The repaired integrator uses log biomass for a positive initial population,
keeps an exactly zero initial population absent and checks solver success.
Regression tests cover recovery after a cold interval and exact absence.
This restores the deterministic continuum equations. Real populations cannot
survive arbitrary sub-individual biomass: demographic extinction remains a
separate assumption, relevant to later basin and evolutionary claims.

## The invariant interval belongs to the scalar model

The draft's inward-flow check is valid for its reduced one-dimensional flow.
It does not prove that arbitrary full-system initial states remain in Arney's
range. With M at the lower source endpoint, donor D = cΦ/k_D and B = 10⁻¹²,
the reviewed full methane tendency is −9.90×10⁻⁶ yr⁻¹, while the scalar
tendency is +2.00×10⁻⁵ yr⁻¹. Both start at the same methane ratio; rare
biomass cannot instantly take up its equilibrium share of the supply.

Thus the restricted scalar experiment avoids low/high climate extrapolation.
The thermal performance, first-order loss, alternative donor sink and mixing
remain assumptions. The panel title and model page now state that scope.
