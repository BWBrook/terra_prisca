# Issue 4: bounded oxidant-source check

Codex (GPT-6), 16 November 2026. Four primary papers were checked against
their full text and live Crossref metadata; identities and response hashes
are in `probe-issue4-source-metadata-2026-11-16.json`. Published estimates
below describe different fluxes and cannot be added into one capacity bound.
No third-party paper is redistributed here.

## Sulfur

**Stüeken, Catling & Buick (2012), Contributions to late Archaean sulphur
cycling by life on land.** DOI [10.1038/ngeo1585](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo1585).
[Author-hosted paper and supplement](https://faculty.washington.edu/dcatling/Stueken2012_SulfurCycling.pdf),
table 1, printed page 2: for 2.8–2.5 Ga, inferred continental sulfur
weathering is **0.55 Tmol S yr⁻¹**, with **0.12 Tmol yr⁻¹** of volcanic sulfur
delivered to continental shelves. This is a continental-margin sulfur budget,
not global gross sulfate reduction. Delivery, speciation and sedimentation
assumptions preclude treating it as measured sulfate supplied to the model's
habitats. It underlies Hao et al.'s sedimentary comparison below; the two
estimates are not independent empirical observations.

**Hao et al. (2022), Anoxic photochemical weathering of pyrite on Archean
continents.** DOI [10.1126/sciadv.abn2226](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abn2226).
[Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9242442/), Discussion's
global-flux paragraphs and tables S1–S2: slow/fast continental-growth models
give new weathering sulfate of **0.064/0.72 Tmol yr⁻¹**, plus volcanic sulfate
of **0.028/0.15 Tmol yr⁻¹** by 2.4 Ga. Combined modeled sources are therefore
**0.092/0.87 Tmol yr⁻¹**, roughly 23–220 times below the reference f = 0.5
scenario's 20.19 Tmol sulfate-equivalent demand. These are late-Archean
source scenarios, not a 2.7-Ga calibration or a cap on recycled sulfate
reduction. Their photochemical mechanism also produces H₂; a planetary
redox budget must include that reductant and any escape.

**Fakhraee & Katsev (2019), Organic sulfur was integral to the Archean sulfur
cycle.** DOI [10.1038/s41467-019-12396-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12396-y).
[Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12396-y), Results,
“Arguments for the significance of organic sulfur”: an assumed
100 Tmol C yr⁻¹ remineralization regenerates **0.3–1 Tmol inorganic S yr⁻¹**.
This includes reduced sulfur and sulfite, and is **not** an estimate of
sulfate supply or sulfate reduction. Pyrite production need not demonstrate
sulfate reduction. Do not use this number one-for-one against sulfate
demand.

## Iron

**Thompson et al. (2019), Photoferrotrophy, deposition of banded iron
formations, and methane production in Archean oceans.** DOI
[10.1126/sciadv.aav2869](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aav2869).
[Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6881150/), “Revised
Precambrian Fe budgets” and table 1: their 2.5-Ga scenarios give an upper
fresh Fe(II) input of about **45 Tmol Fe yr⁻¹**; modeled Fe recycling is
**11.1, 24.8 and 31.4 Tmol Fe yr⁻¹**. The latter matches the iron reduction
associated with modeled organic-carbon remineralization. Eight Fe(III) per
acetate makes the reference demand **161.5 Tmol Fe(III) yr⁻¹**, about 5–15
times those modeled recycling rates. Fresh Fe(II) input is not gross
Fe(III) availability; regeneration can cycle the same iron repeatedly.
Neither flux is an unused oxidant allocation for this model's acetate.
Photoferrotrophy also produces organic reductant, so ferric iron is not a
free net planetary sink. This paper supplies a ferric-reduction precedent,
not a calibrated f or a universal exclusion of local sinks.

## What the sources allow

The reference global acetate sink is demanding relative to these published
new-sulfate and iron-recycling scenarios. They do not determine the fraction
of acetate recovered as methane, its residence time, allocation of oxidants
among substrates, or co-location with hydrogen-fed habitats. Low sulfate
concentration alone cannot supply those missing quantities.

Keep **f near 1 as a conditional closed-recycling scenario**, not as a range
established by the source record. Kharecha et al.'s bulk-burial efficiency is
a model assumption and does not directly calibrate either guild's acetate
or biomass recovery. Oxidant reducers may consume H₂ as well as acetate;
adding them would change the competitive model. That is not part of this
bounded check.

For a stationary regulator, export must ultimately reach a sustained sink
or remain outside the methane system. Finite storage longer than a response
time can change transients, but cannot alone establish a permanent
steady-state methane-yield contrast after eventual complete recovery.
