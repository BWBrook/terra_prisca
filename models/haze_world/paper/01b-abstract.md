Can life regulate its planet's climate when that climate responds slowly? We test this in a model inspired by the hazy early Earth. Two microbial groups compete for hydrogen: methanogens make methane, which forms a cooling organic haze, and acetogens divert hydrogen into acetate. Temperature decides which group grows faster, so their competition can hold temperature exactly constant as hydrogen supply changes. That perfect control is fragile. Of 42 exactly holding states, six were unstable outright and the rest lost stability once the climate took about seven years to respond. Two natural loosenings, acetate slowing its own producers and habitats differing in temperature, made control slightly imperfect and far more durable. A sensitivity of 0.07–0.32 K per e-fold change in supply bought tolerance of a 30-year response, and 1–2 K bought centuries. In invasion simulations, the order of arrival decided which group survived.
