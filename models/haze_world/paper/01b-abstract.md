Can life regulate planetary climate despite its slow response? We examine hydrogen competition between acetogens and methanogens in an Archean-inspired haze model. Methanogens supply methane; acetogens reduce its delivery, thinning the cooling haze. At coexistence, competition can hold temperature exactly as hydrogen supply changes. All 42 exact states were already unstable or first lost stability by about 7.1 years of thermal response time. Acetate self-limitation and habitat-temperature spread broadened stability while allowing residual temperature sensitivity. On four tuned branches, local sensitivities of 0.07–0.32 K per e-fold of supply bought a 30-year stability margin; sampled sensitivities of 1–2 K bought centuries. In 24 closed-habitat invasions with an extinction census, arrival order selected the surviving guild. Within this conditional model, slightly imperfect holding withstands much slower climate adjustment than exact pinning, while assembly and the stability of an established community remain distinct questions.
